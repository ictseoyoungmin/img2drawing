# Croquis

Croquis is fast, selective description of the observed subject.

**Economize marks, not geometry.** Keep the subject's exact flow, silhouette turns, mass
relations, overlaps, negative spaces, contacts, and identity-bearing shape changes while
using as few marks as practical.

Prioritize:
- dominant action and balance;
- head/ribcage/pelvis placement, orientation, and relative twist;
- shoulder/pelvis relation and near/far side read;
- limb direction, joint anchors, and width transitions;
- decisive silhouette and overlap;
- feet/ground and prop/body contact;
- a few exact facial, hair, garment, or footwear lines when they materially identify the
  subject.

## Default value policy

Broad value regions and dense regular hatch fields are **off by default** in croquis. They can
make a weak structural drawing look more finished while hiding errors in turn, twist, overlap,
or limb placement.

Use broad value only when the request explicitly calls for shaded/tonal croquis or the declared
intent materially depends on form/light. Even then, the pose must remain readable when that tone
is mentally removed: major masses, support, limb chains, negative spaces, and prop/body overlap
must already work as structure.

A small local accent is allowed when it clarifies a real observed relation. When a reference's
identity or important form depends on dark hair, garment masses, or soft facial transitions,
selective value marks may carry those observed relationships even in an otherwise line-led
croquis. Preserve the geometry and reserved light; do not treat every soft transition as a
hard outline or fill a dark region with uniform parallel lines. Do not use value to
manufacture missing form or to substitute for unresolved contour ownership.

For identity-bearing dark hair or clothing, distinguish an observed tonal
*region* from the width of a single mark. Map its boundary, plane/clump flow,
and protected highlights first; then layer a small number of deliberately
oriented graphite marks with inspected pressure and density changes.
A painted-black stripe made from overlapping opaque broad strokes is not
evidence of faithful graphite texture or recovered form. See
[`broad-graphite.md`](../markmaking/broad-graphite.md).

Omit redundant search lines, repeated texture, and low-impact shading. Do not replace a
visible head with a circle, limbs with tubes, feet with boxes, or folds with symbols merely
because the drawing is quick.
