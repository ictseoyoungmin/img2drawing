# Balance and limbs

Read limbs as connected chains that transfer weight, gesture, depth, and contact.

## Balance

Identify support side, center-of-mass tendency, ground plane, stance width, and the visual
counterbalance of free limbs or props. Feet are part of the balance solution, not decorative
terminals.

## Limb chains

Track shoulder → elbow → wrist → hand and hip → knee → ankle → foot. Preserve direction,
joint transition, visible width change, and the observed projected spacing between anchors.
Avoid parallel-rail limbs and uniform tubes.

Two side lines connecting one joint to the next are not evidence that a limb is resolved. Before
accepting a limb interval, verify at least one subject-specific relation where the reference allows it:

- taper or width change;
- near/far edge asymmetry;
- joint insertion or overlap;
- bend/rotation cue;
- foreshortened projected spacing;
- garment compression/tension that explains the form;
- terminal orientation or contact.

If none of these survive in the visible drawing, classify the interval as generic rail/tube
shorthand and reopen it rather than adding decorative folds or hatch marks.

A terminal should not be refined until its parent chain is credible. Repeatedly repairing a
hand or foot without changing the parent limb is a signal to re-observe the chain.

When a segment points substantially toward or away from the viewer, do not resolve the
compression with ordinary length/taper assumptions. Route projected length, near/far order,
overlap, and terminal orientation to `foreshortening-and-depth.md`.

For strongly foreshortened views, also verify that perspective propagates through the whole chain.
A large near mass with flat/diagrammatic downstream segments is an upstream depth residual. Use
`../review/visual-quality-gates.md` for the perspective-propagation check.

## Occlusion and negative space

Use negative spaces between limbs/body/props as strong relational evidence. When a limb
passes behind another form, draw the visible handoff and stop where the evidence stops.
Hidden length belongs to the depth relation; do not complete it through the occluder merely
to make the chain look anatomically complete.
