# Balance, joints, limbs and feet

## Balance

First determine which foot supports the figure, which way the pelvis
counterbalances, and whether both feet contact the same ground plane. The
shoulder/pelvis tilt and the negative space between the feet must read together.
A long centreline alone does not solve balance.

## Joint chains

Locate every observable shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, and ankle. A sleeve
cuff is not the wrist, a waistband is not the hip, a jean hem is not the ankle,
and the bottom of a shoe is not the foot. Infer an occluded joint from visible
endpoints and body connections, and state the uncertainty explicitly.

A centre-path curve passes through the joints while preserving tangent changes
and convex/concave turns across the span. Connecting correct joints with straight
segments, or bracketing them with two parallel lines, produces a wire dummy or a
garment outline. Add volume and taper separately in the next representation.

## Feet and occlusion

Foot direction, length, and foreshortening are parts of pose and weight, not late
decoration. Observe the wedge that runs from ankle to ground; defer laces, panels,
and folds. Leave at least a minimal gesture for an arm or hand hidden behind a
prop or garment so the shoulder rhythm and torso turn are not lost.

## Inspectable hypothesis

If the first whole figure does not read as the subject's pose, do not add contour
or detail. Render the current snapshot and view it beside the subject or in a
registered overlay, then distinguish the faulty premise from its responsible
strokes and replace them explicitly.
