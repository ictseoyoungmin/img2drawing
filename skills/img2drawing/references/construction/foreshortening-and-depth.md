# Foreshortening and depth

Use this leaf when a form points substantially toward or away from the viewer and the visible
mismatch depends on projected length, near/far order, overlap, or terminal orientation.

Foreshortening is not a shortened anatomy template. It is the observed projection of a
three-dimensional relation into the drawing.

## Observe the projected relation

Before drawing more contour, identify:

- the visible near and far anchors of the form;
- projected spacing between joints or structural landmarks;
- which segment overlaps or hides another segment;
- apparent width changes supported by the reference;
- the direction and plane of the terminal hand, foot, or attached object;
- negative spaces that confirm the depth order;
- when part of the chain is occluded, the visible entry/reappearance anchors and local direction
  on both sides of the overlap.

A segment may occupy little image-space while representing substantial physical length. Do
not unfold a foreshortened limb to the anatomical length you expect to see.

## Construct from visible anchors and minimum hidden continuity

Place the visible anchors and their projected relations first. Let overlap account for missing
visibility rather than drawing a hidden contour through the occluder.

When the visible anchors on opposite sides of an occlusion belong to one chain or form, infer the
minimum provisional continuation needed to preserve their relation. That inference may include a
centerline tendency, joint-chain continuity, approximate width/mass continuation, or relative
depth. It is not a claim that the hidden contour itself has been observed.

Use the same rule when only one side is visible: preserve enough parent continuity to support the
visible form, but do not invent an exact hidden endpoint, joint location, or silhouette.

Preserve axis changes at visible joints and the orientation of the terminal form; a short upper or
lower segment does not justify turning the hand, foot, or prop toward a convenient default
direction. If a hidden continuation can only connect the visible anchors by introducing an
unsupported sharp bend or moving a correct visible anchor, revisit the parent orientation or
projection premise instead.

Do not apply a formula such as “near means wider” mechanically. Width, taper, and scale change
must follow the actual evidence, lens/perspective effect, clothing, and orientation in the
subject.

## Descriptive consequences

Once the depth premise is credible, use contour and overlap to state it economically:

- foreground edges own the occlusion;
- visible background contour stops at the occluder and resumes only at an observed reappearance;
- the hidden continuation may constrain placement without appearing as a final visible line;
- cross-form or seam cues may clarify plane direction when they are genuinely visible;
- terminal silhouette and contact must agree with the projected chain.

A temporary hidden construction cue may be useful for reasoning, but retire or soften it before it
reads as observed contour. See `../foundation/occlusion-inference.md`.

Adding anatomy detail cannot repair incorrect projected anchors, depth order, or hidden
continuity.

## Escalate or route locally

- several anchors or overlap relations are uncertain → `../observation/visual-observation.md`;
- a bounded angle/distance check would resolve visible-anchor uncertainty → `../observation/measuring-boundaries.md`;
- an occluded interval materially constrains the chain or downstream terminal → `../foundation/occlusion-inference.md`;
- the limb chain/support premise is wrong → `balance-and-limbs.md`;
- only local overlap/contour ownership or reappearance is wrong → `../description/contour-and-overlap.md`;
- a hand/grip terminal is wrong after the parent projection is credible → `../figure/hands-and-grip.md`;
- a foot/footwear terminal is wrong after the parent projection is credible → `../figure/legs-feet.md`;
- a prop axis or body contact contradicts the depth relation → `../props/attached-objects.md`.

When several local parts become “too short” in the same depth direction, treat that as a
projection-premise residual before lengthening each part independently.
