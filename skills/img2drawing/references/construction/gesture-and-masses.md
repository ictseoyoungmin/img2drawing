# Gesture and masses

Construction states a spatial hypothesis for the subject before local description.

## Gesture

Use the dominant action to connect head, torso, pelvis, support, and major limb movement.
The line of action is a directional summary, not a centerline that every body part must
follow.

## Masses

Place head, ribcage, and pelvis as occupied forms with the observed tilt, width, depth,
and overlap. Generic ellipses, beans, or boxes are acceptable only as temporary reasoning
when they still preserve those observed relationships; they are not the target shape.

Keep masses connected by the actual pose. Do not infer a complete hidden torso or limb
through an occluder merely to make construction feel anatomically complete.

A successful construction already reads as this subject's pose at a glance. If it does not,
repair the premise before adding contour or identity accents.
