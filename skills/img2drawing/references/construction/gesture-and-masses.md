# Gesture and primary masses

This document describes how to read and record the drawing's large relationships;
it is not a stage.

## Read the whole figure first

Before the first stroke, briefly observe:

- support side and centre of gravity;
- the dominant flow from head into torso;
- the direction and twist of the head, ribcage, and pelvis;
- the counter-tilt of shoulder and pelvis;
- large silhouette features that distinguish the subject;
- negative spaces between arms and legs;
- ground contact and the axes and overlaps of large props; and
- near/far relationships and occluded endpoints.

Observation is not a ritual of copying a coordinate table. It selects the
relationships the first hypothesis must communicate. Do not hide uncertain
relationships; record them in the observation.

## Ordered, not gated

The suggested authoring grammar may begin whole → flow → masses → balance →
limbs → selected contour. If a later inspection disproves an earlier hypothesis,
correct any item immediately. This order is not a runtime cursor, phase count, or
PASS gate.

## Line of action

Express the body's direction and force with one or two organic flows. The spine,
facial centreline, and limb centre-path carry different information; do not merge
them into one black pole. Begin each arm or leg with one curve through
shoulder–elbow–wrist or hip–knee–ankle. Width and garment contours belong to a
separate, later representation.

## Head and masses

The head is not an oval or a sphere copied from the hair outline. Read the
cranium, jaw turn, chin, and feature-anchored facial centreline first, separating
them from the hair envelope. Treat the ribcage and pelvis as masses with direction,
tilt, overlap, and depth—not width alone. Do not cover an incorrect large rotation
with small detail.
