# Ground and context

Environment exists to support the drawing's spatial reading, not to compete with the
subject unless the request makes environment a primary subject.

Prioritize:
- the ground plane and contact shadows/edges needed to make weight credible;
- major perspective or architectural lines that explain placement;
- essential contextual objects that affect silhouette or overlap;
- atmospheric/value separation when needed for readability.

## Context lines need an owner

Every surviving environment line should correspond to a visible or intentionally constructed
spatial relation: a ground/plane edge, architecture/object edge, contact/shadow boundary, or a
specific scale/depth cue.

Do not add generic perspective rays, arbitrary diagonals, or light scratch fields merely to make
the scene look more complete. A line that cannot answer "which plane/edge/relation owns this?" is
not useful context.

When perspective construction is temporarily helpful, keep it explicitly provisional and retire
or soften it once the spatial relation is carried by stronger visible/context lines.

Do not add background texture while feet, support, or major spatial relations remain
uncertain. Context should clarify scale, depth, and grounding with the smallest sufficient
set of marks.

Use `../review/visual-quality-gates.md` for the context-ownership and retirement checks before
final finish.
