# Contour and overlap

Every contour should have ownership: know what lies on each side of the line.

Use exterior contour for the true visible boundary of the subject or part. Use internal
lines only when they describe overlap, material change, form turning, seam/fold structure,
or an identity-bearing feature.

Do not draw two nearly parallel contours for uncertainty. Re-observe and choose the better
boundary. Do not continue a jaw under hair, a sleeve under a foreground object, or a leg
through another leg unless a visible cue requires it.

At overlaps, prioritize the foreground edge and let the background form disappear cleanly.
The reappearance point matters: it defines depth and should match the observed relation.

Contact lines—foot/ground, hand/prop, garment/body compression—should state where forms
actually meet rather than merely touching symbolically.
