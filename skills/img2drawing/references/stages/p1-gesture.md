# P1 Gesture — flow, balance, centre of gravity

**The question this stage answers: how is this person standing?**

P1 is not an attempt to draw the body accurately. It settles the pose's rhythm and balance
so every later stage has something true to build on.

## What P1 states
- head position and tilt;
- the line of action through the whole body;
- shoulder tilt and pelvis tilt;
- the direction each arm and leg travels;
- where the feet meet the ground;
- the overall silhouette's width and height;
- the major axis of a large attached object, when it changes the envelope.

## How much to draw
A simple head ovoid with a light centre/tilt mark. One dominant line of action. Shoulder
and pelvis lines as tilt cues. Single-line paths for the limbs. Ground marks under the
feet. A loose envelope for the whole figure.

That is the whole stage. Clothing, hair, face, muscle and prop detail must stay out.

## Common failures
- **Several competing sticks instead of one line of action.** The figure has a dominant
  flow; find it before subdividing.
- **Shoulder and pelvis parallel to each other** when the subject's are tilted against each
  other. Tilt carries the weight story.
- **Feet floating.** If the ground contact is unstated, balance cannot be judged.
- **Proportion borrowed from memory.** Head size, shoulder span and pelvis breadth come
  from this subject, not from a remembered generic figure.
- **Drawing the outfit.** A cardigan or a rifle changes the envelope; it does not belong to
  P1 as a shape.

## Hardening order
1. Line of action and balance.
2. Head position and tilt.
3. Shoulder and pelvis tilt.
4. Limb direction paths.
5. Ground contact.
6. Overall envelope check against the subject.
