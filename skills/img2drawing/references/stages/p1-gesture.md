# P1 Gesture — expert guidance

## Dominant construction
The primary gesture starts at the **crown**.

Inside the head, it is a **curved facial centre line**, not a straight geometric bisection. Its relationship with the two head-envelope arcs should create unequal left/right masses and therefore carry face-direction information.

Continue the same dominant intention through:

`crown → curved facial centre → chin → neck → spine → pelvis → support-side hip/leg → weight landing point`

The curve may change direction at the pelvis to express weight transfer. Do not split torso and support leg into independent sticks.

## Subordinate marks
- two open head-envelope arcs;
- shoulder rhythm;
- pelvis rhythm;
- counterbalance-leg flow;
- minimal ground/weight cue;
- large attached-object major axis when it changes the global envelope.

## Common failures discovered in dogfooding
- beginning at neck;
- tiny circular/polygonal head that makes the face axis meaningless;
- straight face centre with no directional cue;
- shoulder/pelvis symbols much smaller than subject proportions;
- vertical torso pole with weak pelvis handoff;
- support and counterbalance legs treated equally;
- pale line hierarchy where no dominant gesture exists;
- cleaning the drawing before the gesture is structurally convincing.

## Hardening order
1. crown + facial direction;
2. chin-to-neck continuity;
3. neck/spine/pelvis rhythm;
4. pelvis-to-support-leg transfer;
5. support vs counterbalance;
6. large proportions;
7. line hierarchy;
8. only then consider advancing.


## Craniofacial gesture hardening

A head envelope is not a low-vertex polygon and not a generic closed oval.

For P1:
- keep the **dominant facial centre** as one smooth crown→forehead→nose/mouth→chin intention;
- use enough sampled points that the renderer does not visibly facet the curve;
- use two **open** cranial/jaw envelope arcs that describe unequal left/right facial masses;
- let the envelope arcs stop at jaw-side / chin-transition regions rather than closing a symbolic egg;
- if a smooth left/right pair still reconstructs a symbolic oval, segment it further: one short crown arc,
  separate temporal→jaw arcs, and visible gaps between crown and side arcs;
- preserve the subject's asymmetry and face turn;
- do not use hair silhouette as a substitute for cranial direction; long hair belongs to P3 mass;
- do not add eyes, nose, lips or other facial-feature detail at P1.

A useful failure test is:
**if the head still reads as a many-sided badge when the rest of the figure is hidden,
P1 is not closed.**
