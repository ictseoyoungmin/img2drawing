# P3 Primary Masses

A rendered example of P3 mass block-in sits beside this file as `p3-primary-masses.png`. Open it if you want to see the
stage's mark-making and detail budget; it shows representation only and never the current
subject's pose or coordinates.

Turn the verified P1 gesture and P2 axes into connected primary masses.

P3 is not a collection of geometric primitives. The objective is one continuous
occupied mass system: head/hair → neck/ribcage → waist bridge → pelvis → limbs.

## Representation boundary
P3 may introduce:
- head/hair mass;
- simple neck-to-ribcage mass bridge;
- ribcage mass contours and a small number of cross-contours;
- pelvis basin/wedge mass contours;
- torso bridge;
- tapered arm/leg masses;
- major attached-object mass when present.

P3 must not introduce:
- articulated shoulder/elbow/knee/ankle blocks;
- hand/foot construction blocks;
- clothing folds;
- facial rendering;
- final cleaned silhouette;
- shading/texture.

## Organic-mass rule
Avoid:
- perfect eggs used as torso substitutes;
- rectangular/box assembly;
- polygonal pelvis wedges;
- parallel-line limb rails.

Prefer:
- asymmetric curved side contours;
- shallow cross-contours that explain volume/tilt;
- pelvis contours that flow into thigh roots;
- limb envelopes that taper along the verified P2 chain;
- overlapping/bridging curves that preserve the P1 rhythm.

## Clothed-mass policy
Do **not** over-infer a hidden naked-body contour when loose clothing dominates the
observation.

For a clothed subject:
- use the observed occupied volume as evidence for the primary mass envelope;
- simplify cardigan/jean bulk into smooth mass contours;
- omit folds, seams, pockets and final clothing-specific silhouette decisions;
- preserve major negative spaces and P2 direction chains.

This is not P5 clothing contour. It is a P3 mass envelope constrained by what is
actually visible.

## Hardening order
1. Preserve P1/P2.
2. Connect head/hair to ribcage through a simple neck bridge.
3. Establish ribcage side contours and one or two useful volume arcs.
4. Build the waist bridge without breaking the spinal rhythm.
5. Build a directional pelvis basin; do not close a horizontal ellipse.
6. Flow pelvis contours into the two thigh roots.
7. Build smooth arm/leg masses around P2 direction chains.
8. For clothed subjects, re-check observed occupied volume before assuming hidden anatomy.
9. Review whole silhouette/negative spaces at mass level.
10. Confirm no P4/P5 vocabulary leaked in.

## Useful local review intents
- `head_neck`: head/hair → neck → ribcage connection;
- `torso_pelvis`: ribcage/waist/pelvis rhythm and cross-contours;
- `shoulders_arms`: mass emergence and taper around arm chains;
- `pelvis_legs`: basin→thigh flow, taper, leg separation and support roles.

## Common failure patterns
Late visual review has exposed:
- egg-like pelvis closure;
- rail-like legs;
- missing major hair mass;
- hidden-body bias that made clothed limbs too thin;
- P3 lines too weak relative to the P1 centreline;
- floating head mass without neck-side bridges.

Therefore clearing a previously listed concern is not enough. Re-observe the whole
current artifact after every major correction; later-order mass failures may become
visible only after earlier errors are removed.

P3 advances only when the figure reads as a connected mass system at a glance and
no major mass/connection mismatch remains.


## Occupied-volume fidelity hardening

When clothing materially changes the observed silhouette, P3 should not collapse back to a
hidden naked-body estimate. Use the **smooth observed occupied volume** as evidence for the
primary mass envelope while still omitting folds, seams, pockets and final clothing contour.

High-impact checks before ADVANCE:
- does the torso mass reflect the visible clothed width rather than only the fitted underlayer?
- do hanging/bent sleeves read as broad tapered masses rather than narrow anatomical rails?
- do trouser legs preserve the subject's broad denim tubes, asymmetry and negative space?
- do the two leg envelopes differ in direction/width according to support vs counterbalance?
- did a correction overshoot outside the observed silhouette while trying to fix an underfilled mass?

A later mass-stage mismatch should be classified before reopen:
- if the inherited P2 axis is wrong, reopen P2;
- if the axis is credible but the P3 envelope underfills/overfills the observed occupied volume,
  correct P3 locally and re-review.

Anti-patterns:
- torso contour hugging the tank-top/body while a loose cardigan materially changes the mass;
- limb pairs as long near-parallel rails with little subject-specific taper;
- widening both sides symmetrically when only one side is wrong;
- solving an underfilled envelope by pushing the opposite side past the silhouette;
- advancing merely because the original concern list is empty without a fresh residual sweep.
