# Facial values and edges

Open when visible facial volumes are positioned correctly but the head still reads as a flat mask,
outlined features, or disconnected shadow patches. This leaf owns facial value/edge description;
[general value principles](../description/value-edge-and-graphite.md) remain the material authority.

## Model the visible planes, not a diagram of anatomy

Observe the broad light family, halftones and shadow families before choosing lines. Compare the
forehead-to-orbit transition, brow shadow, eyelid planes, nose bridge and tip, cheek crest and side
plane, muzzle, chin turn, and jaw-to-neck contact. Preserve differences between near and far sides.
These are observation prompts, not fixed anatomical boundaries or a prescribed lighting setup.
Where the actual reference is softly lit, do not invent hard plane breaks to demonstrate anatomy.

Keep eye whites within the surrounding face value family rather than leaving two white cutouts.
Read the dark upper lid and iris together, but do not darken the whole lower lid to name the eye.
For the nose, separate the small nostril contact from the broad soft wing/bridge transition; avoid
one uninterrupted U-shaped nose-base outline. For the mouth, separate upper-lip value, dark opening,
visible tooth plane, lower-lip light/halftone, and chin transition. Do not draw every tooth with a
full dark outline or use an equally dark lower-lip contour as a second mouth opening.

## Describe skin with restrained contact

Select explicit broad, light strokes for justified turning surfaces, and shorter precise accents
only at visible occlusion/contact. Preserve the observed light planes by controlling overlap,
pressure and opacity rather than drawing bright islands into a solid dark region. Recheck each
transition against neighboring regions; a matched mean darkness can still hide a stripe or step.
Avoid repeated equal-width bands, opaque cheek ovals and outlines around shadows. Use the existing
TonalPatch / TonalAreaPlan and prospective edit inspection when the location of the residual is clear.
Reject a candidate if local continuity improves but the smile, facial width or edge ownership worsens.

## Scale of evidence

Inspect the head crop from the actual current render at the same useful scale as the reference,
then return to the requested output size. Enlargement is observation only; it does not add source
information or authorize inferred microtexture. If the face is small, favor truthful lid, lip,
nostril and cheek relationships over detail that aliases into dark symbols at output scale.
