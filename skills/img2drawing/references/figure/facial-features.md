# Facial features

Open this leaf when the limiting problem is no longer the overall head mass but the instance-specific
shape of the eyes, nose, mouth, or ear.

This leaf owns the **descriptive geometry** of features. It does not replace head construction.
If the features feel pasted into a generic head, reopen [facial construction](facial-construction.md).

## Eyes

Read the eye as an envelope made by lids, canthi, iris visibility, and the neighboring value mass.

Inspect:

- upper-lid arc and lower-lid arc as separate specific curves;
- inner and outer corner heights;
- iris-to-lid overlap and how much sclera is truly visible;
- thickness of the upper-lid shadow band;
- relation of the eye to the brow, nose bridge, and cheek plane.

Avoid:

- equal almond symbols for both eyes;
- fully exposed circular irises when the lids actually cover them;
- eyelashes used to compensate for incorrect lid placement.

## Brows

Brows are feature masses and directional accents, not floating decorative strokes.

Compare:

- start height near the glabella;
- arch location;
- taper and terminal direction;
- relation to the orbit band and expression.

If the eye reads wrong, do not decorate the brow first.

## Nose

The nose is mostly a turning value family with a small number of decisive edges.

Compare:

- bridge direction and width;
- tip projection;
- nostril and wing asymmetry;
- nose base tilt;
- soft value transition into the cheek and philtrum.

Do not force a hard contour where the reference only shows a soft value turn.

## Mouth and smile

Read the mouth together with the muzzle, corners, and surrounding value shifts.

Compare:

- mouth width against eye spacing and lower-face width;
- corner heights and their directional asymmetry;
- upper/lower lip thickness pattern;
- visible teeth as a single value family before separating individual tooth suggestions;
- shadow under the lower lip and its width.

A smile often lifts the corners and compresses the cheeks. If the lips alone look correct but the
smile still feels wrong, reopen the cheek / muzzle relation.

## Ear

Only draw the ear information the reference clearly supports.

Compare:

- height range against brow and nose base;
- tilt;
- outer silhouette;
- lobe relation;
- overlap with hair and neck.

Do not replace an indistinct ear with a diagrammatic template.

## Hierarchy

A few precise feature lines are better than many weak symbolic lines. Give priority to the lines or
value edges that most strongly differentiate this face from a generic one.

For small faces, compare both eyes independently with the same scale. Explicitly compare lid aperture
height at the iris, iris width and exposed height, the dark lash boundary, and the inner-to-outer
corner slope. Use distinct near/far sides and avoid a hard loop around the lower lid. At the mouth,
separate the contact shadows in the corners from the tooth-to-lip boundary; a dark continuous line
around a white crescent usually reads as a symbol rather than a specific smile. Route soft transitions
to [facial values](facial-values.md) rather than increasing outline weight.
