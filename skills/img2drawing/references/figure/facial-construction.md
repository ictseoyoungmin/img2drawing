# Facial construction

The face is a turning front attached to a larger head volume. Do not treat it as a flat feature map
inside a generic oval.

## Purpose

Open this leaf when likeness depends on the face reading as a specific three-dimensional form rather
than a symbolic arrangement of eyes, nose, and mouth.

This leaf owns:

- the head-to-face handoff;
- the front/side plane logic of the face;
- the brow/orbit/cheek/jaw/chin scaffold;
- the relation between visible construction and later descriptive geometry.

It does **not** authorize hidden anatomy or canonical proportions. Preserve the observed instance.

## Construction relationships

Use the smallest construction that helps the observed face read more specifically. These are revisitable hypotheses, not mandatory stages. A visible relation can reopen any earlier premise.

```text
1. cranial mass and head tilt/turn
2. brow/orbit band and face cross-axis
3. cheek + muzzle / mouth block + chin/jaw relation
4. feature seats (not feature symbols)
5. retire or soften obsolete guides
```

### 1. Cranial mass and head tilt/turn

First preserve the actual crown, forehead arc, temple turn, cheek fall, jaw width, chin width, and
neck handoff. The cranial mass may begin as a provisional sphere/egg, but the final head may not
remain a generic oval if the reference is more specific.

Read:

- cranial height against jaw height;
- visible side-plane width;
- tilt of the brow-to-chin direction;
- head turn against neck and shoulders;
- asymmetry caused by expression, camera, or hairstyle.

### 2. Brow/orbit band and face cross-axis

Use the face cross-axis only to explain orientation. It must agree with the observed brow band,
visible side plane, and chin direction.

For likeness-bearing faces, also read the orbit band as a continuous structure rather than two
isolated eye holes. The eyes sit inside this band; they are not pasted onto a blank front plane.

Check:

- inner/outer brow heights;
- near/far orbit exposure;
- eye line tilt versus head tilt;
- the width and turning of the glabella / upper nose root.

### 3. Cheek, muzzle, and chin-jaw relation

Before detailing the mouth or nostrils, inspect the supporting front volumes:

- cheek fullness under the eyes;
- side plane to front-plane handoff;
- muzzle / mouth volume relation (only use a cylinder or wedge as a provisional aid);
- chin projection and width;
- jaw descent into the neck.

A smile often changes the cheek and muzzle more than it changes the line of the lips. If the smile
reads wrong, reopen the cheek–muzzle relation before redrawing the mouth shape repeatedly.

### 4. Feature seats before feature lines

Each feature occupies a seat inside the head construction.

- **Eyes:** bounded by lids within the orbit band; compare the eye envelope, not just the iris.
- **Nose:** rooted at the brow/glabella and turning into the muzzle; compare bridge, tip, wings,
  and base as one turning form.
- **Mouth:** sits on the muzzle; compare width, corner direction, upper/lower lip proportion, and
  mouth-to-chin distance.
- **Ear:** compare height range, tilt, and handoff to jaw/temple rather than drawing a template ear.

If a feature seems misplaced, reopen its seat and neighboring anchors before polishing the feature
itself.

## Portrait-specific checks

For likeness-bearing portrait work, inspect the drawing at both useful whole-head and enlarged-face
scales. Compare the current drawing and reference with explicit anchors.

Optional anchor groups, chosen from actual visible evidence:

```text
head width       = left cheek / temple envelope ↔ right cheek / temple envelope
head height      = crown ↔ chin
eye spacing      = left inner corner ↔ right inner corner against face width
mouth position   = mouth corners / philtrum against nose base and chin
jaw relation     = cheek turn ↔ jaw angle ↔ chin width
hair handoff     = forehead / temple / cheek / ear / neck ownership
```

Do not normalize toward an average face. Use the observed face as the authority.

## Construction retirement

Before final description, classify face guides:

- **KEEP** if a guide still clarifies the requested finish;
- **SOFTEN** if it remains useful but must stay subordinate;
- **RETIRE** if descriptive geometry now carries the information.

A portrait can fail likeness even when the feature details are neat if the head scaffold underneath is
still generic.
