# Head, face, and hair

The head is a shaped volume with identity, not a circle that receives feature symbols.

## Head silhouette

Read crown, forehead, brow/temple turn, cheek, jaw, chin, ear region, and neck handoff as
one connected silhouette. Preserve the observed asymmetry, tilt, foreshortening, and
cranial-to-jaw proportion.

In sparse gesture work, facial features may be omitted, but head direction may not disappear with
them. Preserve at least enough crown/face/jaw relation, side-plane indication, or face-axis evidence
to keep the head from reading as an orientationless circle. A cranial search sphere is temporary
reasoning; if it remains visible in the final gesture, reshape or retire it unless the subject truly
supports that circular silhouette.

## Identity-fidelity reminder

Canonical authority, including supplied-instance-over-remembered-design, lives in
`../foundation/reference-authority.md`.

For this leaf, do not redraw a familiar or named subject from memory. Preserve the supplied head
shape, feature spacing, hair silhouette/parting, visible accessories, and styling unless the user
explicitly requests a transformation. If identity evidence is uncertain, reopen observation rather
than substituting a canonical model-sheet version.

## Face and likeness routing

When likeness is important, including a readable face in a full-figure drawing, open
[portrait likeness review](../review/portrait-likeness.md) before accepting facial description or
finishing. Use its local-scale evidence instead of full-figure normalization. Open the specialist
owner for the actual residual:

- [Facial construction](facial-construction.md): face plane, orbit, cheek, jaw and feature seats.
- [Facial features](facial-features.md): instance-specific lids, iris visibility, nose, lips and ear.
- [Facial expression](facial-expression.md): linked changes in lids, cheeks, mouth and jaw.
- [Facial values](facial-values.md): skin planes, occlusion accents and soft transitions.

Inspect the current face and whole head before refining feature symbols. Reopen the underlying
volume or orientation when feature geometry cannot explain the observed likeness.

## Hair

Hair is solved in three levels:

```text
1. head + hair outer mass
2. major parting and clump boundaries
3. selected strand accents
```

Do not enter level 3 until levels 1–2 survive a whole-head inspection.

First block the hair as a mass that changes the head silhouette and establishes ownership at the
forehead, temple, cheek/jaw, ear, neck, shoulder, and garment handoffs that are actually visible.
Then add only the major parting and clump boundaries needed to explain overlap/direction/identity.
Only after that may a small number of strand accents clarify flow or material.

### Clump volume before shine

For curls, waves, or other visibly turning clumps, compare a few actual major overlaps:
where the clump starts or emerges, its direction changes, its visible width, the next clump
that covers it, and where it disappears. Use temporary centerlines or asymmetric cross-sections
only where they help test the observed volume; do not invent closed tubes or hidden curls.

Read the broad light-facing surface, the turning halftone, and the darker side/contact shadow
as related value families. Follow their changes along the clump and across its visible width
(see [value continuity](../description/value-edge-and-graphite.md)). Preserve the broad light
reserve during deposition, with darker marks owned by actual turns or overlap. Avoid giving
each curl the same black outline, bright central ribbon, or rounded light ends. A narrow lift
or fine strand is a subordinate material accent, not the construction of a clump. Inspect the
whole head with major values alone before accepting such accents; if separate overlapping
volumes do not read, retire the obstructing marks and correct the parent clump geometry/value.

Do not fill the head with parallel strand lines. If removing the strand accents destroys the hair
read, the mass/clump solution was never closed. Delete or retire the strand field and reopen the
outer mass/parting/clump relation.

A hair stroke may not continue into an arm, collar, shoulder, or background merely because the
screen-space tangent aligns. Split at the physical ownership handoff. See
`../review/visual-quality-gates.md`.

## Line economy and retirement

When the head becomes muddy, remove search lines first. Re-establish one clean outer shape
and a small set of internal identity lines before adding anything else.

Before final finish, classify surviving cranial spheres, face axes, hair search arcs, and strand
accents as KEEP / SOFTEN / RETIRE. Construction that has already been replaced by descriptive
geometry should not remain visible by default.
