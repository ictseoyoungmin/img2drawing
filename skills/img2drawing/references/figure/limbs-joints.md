# Joints, limbs and body chains

Find the relationship, not a generic mannequin.

## Joint chain

Locate shoulder → elbow → wrist and hip → knee → ankle from visible anatomical evidence.
Then connect each chain with a curved centre path that preserves tangent changes,
foreshortening, and convex/concave turns. A correct set of points joined by straight
segments still becomes a flat wire dummy.

Clothing boundaries are traps: a sleeve cuff is not the wrist, a waistband is not the
hip, and a jean hem is not the ankle. Use visible skin, crotch, shoe outline, and body
connection to infer hidden centres; write uncertainty when the endpoint is occluded.

## Volume and taper

After the pose chain reads, add limb volume and taper around the observed path. Do not
use two parallel lines as a substitute for a limb's direction when the purpose is a first
construction hypothesis. A trouser or sleeve contour must hang from the underlying joint
chain rather than define it.

## Feet and occlusion

Foot direction and ground contact carry weight and body direction. Use a wedge or compact
shape that matches foreshortening, not a repeated oval. An arm or leg hidden behind a prop
or garment still needs a minimum gesture inferred from shoulder/elbow/wrist or
hip/knee/ankle evidence.

## Correction

If later inspection shows a wrong premise, replace the responsible explicit strokes and
inspect again. Do not preserve a false silhouette by adding detail around it, and do not
create a stage transition to justify the correction.
