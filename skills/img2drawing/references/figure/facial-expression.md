# Facial expression

Open this leaf when the drawing is recognizable as a face but the lived expression still does not
match the reference.

Expression is a relationship problem. Do not isolate the mouth or eyes from the rest of the face.

## Read expression as linked changes

Observe how these areas change together:

```text
brows ↔ lids ↔ cheeks ↔ nose base ↔ mouth corners ↔ chin / jaw tension
```

A smile, for example, may cause (check the actual reference, never impose these changes):

- mouth corners to lift and widen;
- cheek masses to rise and round;
- lower lids to compress slightly;
- nasolabial value or fold direction to change;
- the chin-to-lower-lip relation to shorten visually.

## Suggested observation passes

1. **Whole face:** what is the dominant expression and which regions carry it?
2. **Anchor comparison:** compare eye corners, mouth corners, nose base, chin, and cheek crest.
3. **Value comparison:** check whether the soft value changes around the eyes, nose, and mouth agree
   with the line drawing.
4. **Reopen parent form if needed:** if the expression feels stiff, the problem may belong to the
   cheek, muzzle, or head tilt rather than the lip contour.

## Common failure modes

- a smiling mouth on a neutral cheek structure;
- symmetrical eyes and brows on an asymmetrical expression;
- overdrawn teeth that ignore the mouth opening shape;
- dark outline under the mouth substituting for lower-face volume;
- expression reduced to eyebrow symbols.

## Acceptance check

Before accepting an expression-bearing face, verify that the same expression still reads when:

- strand accents are muted;
- the mouth is inspected together with cheeks and eyes;
- the face is viewed at both whole-head and enlarged-face scales.

If the expression only reads in a tight crop or only because of one graphic symbol, reopen the
supporting facial structure.
