# Clothing folds

Clothing folds are evidence of force, drape, construction, and contact. They are not
surface decoration.

For each important fold, identify its cause:
- tension between anchors;
- compression at a joint or contact;
- gravity/drape from a support;
- seam or garment construction;
- body/prop contact.

Draw fewer folds, but keep their observed origin, direction, length, and termination. A
small number of correctly placed folds can describe material and pose better than dense
wrinkle noise.

Avoid repeated zigzags, evenly spaced V-shapes, or generic hatching. If a fold does not
change form reading, material, or identity, it is usually lower priority than a missing
silhouette or overlap relationship.

## Do not decorate unresolved tubes

Sleeves and trouser legs must already preserve their parent chain, taper/width changes, overlap,
and orientation before folds are used to enrich them. Do not add crease/hatch marks merely to make
a rail-like or tube-like limb look more finished.

## Hatch needs a causal owner

Clothing hatch marks are justified only when they communicate a value family, plane turn,
material direction, compression/tension, contact/cast shadow, or an explicitly requested style.
Random faint scratches are not evidence of cloth detail.

Before finish, verify that each surviving fold/hatch family has a readable cause and remains
subordinate to silhouette/overlap structure. Use `../review/visual-quality-gates.md` for the
anti-symbol, hierarchy, and retirement audits.
