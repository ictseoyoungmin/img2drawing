# Legs and feet

Lower-body accuracy depends on changing widths, joint transitions, stance geometry, and
contact with the ground.

## Legs

Preserve hip exit, thigh contour, knee break, calf bulge/taper, shin direction, ankle
narrowing, and foreshortening. The two sides of a leg rarely behave like parallel rails.
Do not reduce the limb to a uniform tube.

Compare both legs through pelvis position, stance width, overlap, and the negative space
between them. A small error in hip or knee placement can make both feet appear wrong.

## Feet and footwear

Resolve ankle → heel → sole → toe direction as a load-bearing shape. Preserve footwear
upper, opening/cuff, sole thickness, toe profile, heel position, and visible perspective
when present. Do not use a generic rectangle, wedge, or rounded block as the final foot.

Ground contact must be explicit: know which region bears weight, where the sole meets the
plane, and how the foot direction supports the pose. A floating or penetrating foot is a
structural residual, not a cosmetic one.
