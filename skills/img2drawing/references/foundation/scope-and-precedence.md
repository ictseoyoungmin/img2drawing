# Scope and precedence

Work on the smallest scope that can actually fix the visible problem, but choose the
problem by perceptual impact.

## Priority order

1. pose, composition, occupied mass, support, and balance;
2. silhouette, negative space, overlap, grounding, and major object relations;
3. part geometry and identity-bearing structure;
4. value/edge organization and material description;
5. accents and texture.

A local issue may require a larger correction. If a shoe looks wrong because the lower leg
axis is wrong, repair the leg. If the face looks wrong because the head orientation is
wrong, repair the head construction. Do not preserve a bad premise merely because the
visible symptom is small.

## No stage ownership

These scopes are not stages. A later observation may invalidate an earlier construction at
any time. Route backward immediately rather than hiding the mismatch under cleanup.

## Correction budget

Fix one to three highest-impact residuals per inspection turn. This keeps cause and effect
readable and prevents many low-confidence edits from accumulating into noise.
