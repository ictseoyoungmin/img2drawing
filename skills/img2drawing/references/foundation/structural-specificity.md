# Structural specificity

**Defer secondary detail, not structural specificity.**

An early pass may omit low-impact local information, but it must still state the subject's
specific structure. Structural information is defined by the relationships it controls, not by
whether a feature is large or small.

## Preserve from the first useful construction

When observation supports them, preserve:

- placement and occupied extent;
- major orientation, turn, and directional axes;
- relative proportion and spacing;
- characteristic envelope and width/depth changes;
- major overlap, contact, and negative space;
- anchor or connected-part relationships;
- support, balance, or grounding when they materially define the subject.

These relations may be stated with sparse marks, but they may not be replaced by generic circles,
boxes, beans, capsules, rails, tubes, or other convenience symbols that change the observed
structure.

## Reasoning primitives are disposable

A construction primitive is allowed only as temporary reasoning. It does not become valid visible
geometry by default.

If a circle, ellipse, bean, capsule, box, wedge, polygon, or other shorthand remains in the drawing
after its reasoning job is done, ask whether its visible shape still preserves the subject's actual
direction, volume, asymmetry, attachment, and transitions. If not, retire or reshape it.

For organic figure construction in particular:

- head direction must not collapse into an orientationless circle when crown/face/profile/jaw/nape
  information is readable;
- ribcage turn should preserve observed shoulder/back/side asymmetry, taper, cross-direction,
  overlap, and width change rather than arbitrary hard facets **or** a stock rounded bean/capsule;
- pelvis should preserve observed hip shelf, near/far relation, width transition, and leg-attachment
  spacing/directions rather than a flat triangle/polygon/plate **or** a smooth oval/capsule;
- `rounded` is not automatically more specific than `angular`: a smooth mass that could fit many
  unrelated subjects is still generic shorthand;
- hard corners and plane breaks belong only where the observed subject or declared design actually
  supports them, while real asymmetry should not be smoothed away for visual neatness.

Sparse drawing remains specific when each retained mark carries real structural information.
Geometric or smoothly organic simplification that changes the subject into stock construction
symbols is not line economy.

## What may be deferred

Secondary detail may be postponed when it does not decide the structural read, including:

- texture and surface noise;
- decorative motifs or markings;
- repeated small features;
- minor seams, grooves, fasteners, strands, or folds;
- micro-accents that do not establish placement, orientation, proportion, overlap, contact, or
  identity-bearing form.

A small feature is not automatically secondary. If it determines orientation, contact, scale,
identity, or another high-impact relation, it belongs to the current structural problem.

## Construction is provisional

Construction primitives are reasoning aids, not geometry authority. A mark does not earn
preservation merely because it was drawn earlier or because later marks were added around it.

Before a descriptive pass inherits existing construction, compare the whole drawing against its
reference authority or declared intent again. If the parent structure is still credible, retain it
and describe further. If later observation disproves it, retire or replace the responsible marks
instead of refining around the mistake.

```text
previous construction
→ fresh whole-subject comparison
→ parent structure still credible?
   ├─ yes → retain and describe further
   └─ no  → replace responsible geometry first
```

This is not a stage gate. Revalidation can happen at any time in the same
`observe → draw → inspect → correct` loop.
