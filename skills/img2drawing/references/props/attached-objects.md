# Attached objects and props

Treat an attached object as real geometry with its own axis, thickness, topology, and
contacts—not as a line laid over the figure.

Establish:
- dominant axis and perspective;
- major volume/thickness changes;
- distinctive topology and component breaks;
- which body parts pass in front or behind;
- grip, sling, strap, pocket, or support contacts;
- where the object's silhouette materially changes the subject silhouette.

## Solve object and body together

Do not finish the object independently and then place it beside the figure. The prop/body relation
is one structural problem. Verify the object's principal axis and thickness against the same body
anchors that support or occlude it.

Before accepting the relation, check:

- at least two body/object anchors when visible;
- front/behind ownership at crossings;
- actual grip/strap/support contact rather than near-contact;
- negative spaces created by the object and body;
- continuation through occlusion without drawing hidden appearance as visible;
- scale against nearby torso/limb landmarks;
- whether component circles/rectangles/rails describe real topology or merely symbolize it.

For long props, small angular errors propagate across the whole composition. Check the
object against both hands/body anchors rather than correcting only one endpoint.

Do not let the object pierce the body accidentally or float away from the hand/contact that
supports it.

If the object reads mainly as two rails plus generic circles/boxes, route back to topology,
thickness, and contact rather than adding more component symbols. Use
`../review/visual-quality-gates.md` for the prop/body and anti-symbol audits.
